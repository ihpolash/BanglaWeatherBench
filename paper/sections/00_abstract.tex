Time-series foundation models promise that you can pretrain once and then forecast any series without fitting.
The evidence comes almost entirely from mid-latitude, data-rich places, and often from reanalysis rather than
from instruments. BanglaWeatherBench tests that promise in a tropical monsoon climate, against what stations
actually recorded. We compare 8 zero-shot foundation models with 10 trained and statistical baselines at 35
stations of the Bangladesh Meteorological Department, using European stations, co-located reanalysis and
gap-matched contexts as controls. Foundation models generally rank above the untuned baselines. Yet with the best
of each chosen on earlier years, 26 of 48 corrected tests cannot tell them apart. No model beats a day-of-year
climatology on tropical rainfall beyond a one-day lead. For rainfall, temperature and humidity, reanalysis looks
more predictable than the stations it stands in for, so benchmarks built on it overstate skill. A long-lead
temperature deficit at ten Bangladeshi stations is mostly explained by sparser records rather than climate.
Across about 30 monsoon spells, skill is strong in breaks and worse than climatology in active spells, when
forecasts matter most. The protocol transfers; the numbers, from one country, may not.
