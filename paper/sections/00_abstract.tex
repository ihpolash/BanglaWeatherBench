Time-series foundation models promise that you can pretrain once and then forecast any series without fitting.
The evidence comes almost entirely from mid-latitude, data-rich places, and often from reanalysis rather than
from instruments. BanglaWeatherBench asks what that promise is worth in a tropical monsoon climate, measured
against what stations actually recorded. We compare 8 zero-shot foundation models with 10 trained and statistical
baselines at 35 stations of the Bangladesh Meteorological Department. Three controls help distinguish climatic effects
from differences in the observational data: European stations from the same network, reanalysis at the same coordinates, and contexts given
Bangladesh's gaps in the record. Foundation models generally rank above the untuned,
fixed-configuration baselines. Yet with the best of each chosen on earlier years and the tests corrected for
multiple comparisons, the two cannot be told apart in 26 of 48 tests. No model beats a
day-of-year climatology on tropical rainfall at these stations beyond a one-day lead. For rainfall, temperature and humidity,
reanalysis looks more predictable than the stations it stands in for, so benchmarks built on it overstate skill. A long-lead temperature deficit in
Bangladesh is mostly explained by sparser station records rather than by climate, though it rests on ten stations.
Within the monsoon, across about 30 spell events, skill is strong in break spells and worse than climatology in active ones, when forecasts
matter most. These results come from one country's stations. The protocol transfers; the numbers may not. The
whole leaderboard reproduces in 4.16 GPU-hours on a Tesla T4.
