\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The limits below are ordered by how much each restricts the paper's main claims, and each names what it prevents
us from concluding.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The baselines are untuned, and the comparators are chosen on five years.} Each baseline runs with
    one fixed configuration, so we cannot claim that foundation models match or beat \emph{tuned} baselines; a
    tuning budget could close some of the 17 margins they win. The comparators are chosen on the 2011--2015
    validation years and frozen before testing, so test-period scores do not enter the selection. Five years is a
    short window for choosing among near-tied models, however. The validation-chosen and test-chosen pairs agree in
    only 17 of 48 cases. No verdict reverses between the two choices, though 7 of 48 move between significant and indistinguishable.
    Which model counts as ``best'' for a
    given task is not, so we do not rank individual models on the strength of these pairs. LightGBM's validation
    scores are optimistic; excluding it from the selection changes one verdict.
  \item \textbf{The equity contrast rests on ten stations.} The tropical-versus-temperate comparison uses the 10
    GHCN-Daily stations in Bangladesh: 1{,}154 rainfall and 1{,}478 temperature windows, an order of magnitude
    fewer than the BMD track. Its intervals are unadjusted, and its control is a single baseline selected on test
    data. We therefore cannot claim that the long-lead temperature deficit is established for Bangladesh as a
    whole, or that it would survive correction. The same-network reference, the like-for-like gap control and
    origin-level resampling limit the risk; they do not remove it. We report the contrast because it is the only
    like-for-like comparison available, not because ten stations settle the question.
  \item \textbf{One country, one climate.} Every number comes from Bangladesh's monsoon regime, so we cannot claim
    any of them for other tropical networks. The protocol, controls and significance machinery transfer; whether
    the deficit or the spell reversal appears elsewhere is open.
  \item \textbf{Secondary analyses are unadjusted.} The reanalysis contrast (108 intervals), the spell contrasts
    (72) and the skill intervals (432) are per-comparison. Their counts describe a pattern; no single interval in
    them is a corrected significance result. The spell contrasts also rest on few independent events, 11 or 12
    active and 18 to 21 break, so they cannot be read as station-day sample sizes.
  \item \textbf{Zero-shot only.} We evaluate models as shipped. Few-shot adaptation and light fine-tuning are out of
    scope, so this paper bounds the zero-shot claim, not the models' ceiling.
  \item \textbf{Imputation in the source record.} The published BMD data was imputed by its authors before release.
    We flag suspect values and exclude flagged targets, but detection is imperfect: flagged shares over the test
    period run from \SI{0.08}{\percent} (rainfall) to \SI{4.44}{\percent} (humidity), with one station-variable
    at \SI{14.1}{\percent}. Undetected imputed values would make targets smoother, so we cannot rule out a small
    advantage for smooth forecasters on the BMD track.
  \item \textbf{Two comparisons are weaker than the rest.} Bright sunshine hours and downward shortwave radiation are
    not the same physical quantity, so that row of the reanalysis contrast is reported but not relied on. The
    CHIRPS dekadal task is an independent-source check at coarse resolution, not a headline.
  \item \textbf{Mechanisms are hypothesised, not tested.} We measure the regime reversal and the
    context-availability effect; we do not identify the architectural cause of either.
\end{enumerate}

The roster will also age. Eight models current in 2026 will not stay current, and model checkpoints and
packages change; the versions we used are listed under Reproducibility. The harness and scoring code are released
so that adding a model is a single run against unchanged windows.
