\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We built a benchmark for daily weather forecasting on tropical-monsoon station observations, with the controls
needed to interpret a tropical result: a same-network temperate reference, a reanalysis track at identical
coordinates, and a gap-matched context control applied to models and baselines alike. We evaluated 8 zero-shot
time-series foundation models against 10 trained and statistical baselines over 170{,}571 rolling-origin windows,
treating the forecast origin as the unit of resampling.

The picture is mixed. Foundation models generally rank above the fixed-configuration baselines we evaluated. On
station observations, though, most of that advantage is not statistically separable: with comparators chosen on
earlier years, the best foundation model wins 5 of 24 station comparisons. Nothing beats a day-of-year
climatology on tropical rainfall beyond a one-day lead. For rainfall, temperature and humidity, reanalysis produces more
favourable apparent skill than the stations it represents, though not for temperature at a 30-day lead. Across
about 30 spell events, skill reverses between monsoon break and active spells. The long-lead tropical temperature deficit that half the roster shows is mostly, though not
entirely, a consequence of sparser station context rather than of the tropics; it is also the least certain of
these findings, resting on ten stations.

For practitioners in data-sparse regions the practical reading is twofold. Zero-shot models are already
affordable and already competitive on smooth variables; for rainfall, a well-built climatology remains the thing
to beat. For benchmark builders the reading is that scoring target, regime stratification and context availability
are not implementation details: each one changed a conclusion in this study. All of these numbers come from one
country's stations, and only the protocol is known to transfer.

Three extensions would sharpen the picture. Tuned baselines would test whether the margins we report hold against the strongest available baseline. Few-shot adaptation is cheap for
several models in the roster and would test whether the gaps are properties of zero-shot use or of the models
themselves. Running the same protocol on a second tropical network would show whether the long-lead temperature
deficit is Bangladeshi or tropical. The harness, splits and aggregated score tables are released so that each is a
run against unchanged windows.
