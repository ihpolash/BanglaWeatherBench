# High-Novelty arXiv Paper Topics for a Bangladesh Weather/Climate Dataset (Single-GPU, 4–6 Weeks)

## TL;DR
- **Your single best bet is a benchmark paper — "BanglaWeatherBench": the first systematic zero-shot/few-shot evaluation of time-series foundation models (Chronos, TimesFM, Moirai, Tiny Time Mixers, Moment) against classical baselines on tropical-monsoon Bangladesh station data.** It is inference-only (fits one consumer GPU), uses data you already hold, targets a documented literature gap (no monsoon/Global-South TSFM benchmark exists), and doubles as a citable dataset release — the ideal shape for a 4–6 week preprint.
- **Two other high-novelty, low-compute options** are (a) an independent third-party audit of Google Flood Hub forecasts against FFWC/BWDB ground truth in Bangladesh, and (b) an ML study of wet-bulb/WBGT heat-humidity compound extremes — both sit in genuinely thin literature and structurally avoid the "just an LSTM on a local dataset" reviewer trap.
- **Avoid the crowded lanes**: generic LSTM rainfall forecasting, flood-susceptibility mapping with Random Forest/XGBoost for Bangladesh, and post-event Sentinel-1 inundation mapping of the 2022 Sylhet flood are all saturated with marginal-gain papers that reviewers now reject on novelty grounds.

## Key Findings

**1. The foundation-model-in-the-tropics benchmark gap is real and single-GPU-feasible.** Time-series foundation models — Chronos (Ansari et al., 2024, arXiv:2403.07815), TimesFM (Das et al., 2024, ICML), Moirai (Woo et al., 2024, ICML), Tiny Time Mixers/TTM (Ekambaram et al., 2024, NeurIPS), Moment (Goswami et al., 2024, ICML), Lag-Llama (Rasul et al., 2023), and TimeGPT (Garza & Mergenthaler-Canseco, 2023) — are all released with pretrained weights that run zero-shot on a single consumer GPU. General zero-shot suites (GIFT-Eval; the Monash Time Series Forecasting Archive, Godahewa et al., 2021) include some weather series but are not monsoon-region-specific. There is no dedicated published benchmark isolating tropical-monsoon or South-Asian data-scarce station performance. This is a defensible, clearly-stated novelty claim and the safest path to a fast, citable preprint.

**2. Global weather foundation models have documented precipitation/convection failure modes directly relevant to Bangladesh.** WeatherBench 2 (Rasp et al., 2024, JAMES) documents that deterministic models like GraphCast (Lam et al., 2023, Science) and Pangu-Weather (Bi et al., 2023, Nature) over-smooth precipitation extremes and tend to under-deepen tropical cyclones — a direct concern for Bay-of-Bengal systems. Pre-monsoon Kalbaishakhi/nor'wester convection is mesoscale and largely unresolved at the ~0.25° grid these models use. A Bay-of-Bengal / Bangladesh-specific audit of these failure modes appears to be thin/emerging and is a legitimate secondary framing, though a full gridded-model audit is heavier than a station-level TSFM benchmark.

**3. No CAMELS-BD exists, and the original Caravan does not cover the Ganges-Brahmaputra-Meghna (GBM) basin.** Caravan (Kratzert, Nearing, Addor et al., 2023, Scientific Data) harmonizes CAMELS-US, CAMELS-GB, CAMELS-BR, CAMELS-CL, CAMELS-AUS, HYSETS, and LamaH-CE into ERA5-Land-forced, common-format catchments — concentrated in North America, Europe, South America, and Australia. Bangladesh/GBM catchments are absent. A CAMELS-style Bangladesh contribution would be high-impact and highly citable, but it is bottlenecked by the fact that FFWC publishes river *water levels* (stage), not *discharge*, and true CAMELS-style datasets are built on gauged streamflow. Verify separately whether the recent CAMELS-IND (India) release includes any trans-boundary Ganges/Brahmaputra catchments before committing.

**4. No independent audit of Google Flood Hub against FFWC ground truth appears to exist.** Nearing et al. (2024, Nature 627:559–563, "Global prediction of extreme floods in ungauged watersheds") evaluate their LSTM-based global model against the GRDC global gauge database and reanalysis — not against FFWC-specific Bangladesh gauges. Google has run operational/anticipatory-action collaborations in Bangladesh (with RIMES and humanitarian actors), but those are partner reports, not adversarial third-party verification. An independent skill-scoring of Flood Hub against FFWC observations is highly novel and needs almost no training compute — but it lives or dies on data access.

**5. Wet-bulb/WBGT ML for Bangladesh is thin and data-rich for you.** Raymond, Matthews & Horton (2020, Science Advances, DOI 10.1126/sciadv.aaw1838) flag the Ganges basin/South Asia as a global wet-bulb hotspot. Your `All_Districts_Combined.csv` already contains every WBGT input — T2M, T2MDEW, RH2M, QV2M, ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN, WS2M. Dedicated ML prediction/characterization of WBGT extremes for Bangladesh is sparse, making this a genuine, data-ready gap.

**6. Several Bangladesh ML lanes are crowded and should be avoided.** Flood-susceptibility mapping (RF/XGBoost/ensemble), generic LSTM rainfall forecasting, and post-event Sentinel-1 inundation mapping of the June 2022 Sylhet/Sunamganj haor flood are all saturated. Predictive early-warning ML for the haor flash floods (as opposed to post-event mapping) is thinner, but is constrained by the availability of sub-daily river/rainfall observations for that specific region.

## Details

### FFWC scraping feasibility (verify live before relying on this)
FFWC operates under the Bangladesh Water Development Board (BWDB), Ministry of Water Resources. The dashboard at `ffwc.gov.bd/app/home` is a single-page JavaScript application that fetches data from backend JSON endpoints observable in the browser's network/developer tab; the legacy `ffwc.gov.bd` site publishes daily HTML station tables and PDF/image flood bulletins during the monsoon season. FFWC monitors on the order of 90–110 water-level stations, of which roughly 50+ are formal forecast points with defined Danger Levels, and it issues operational 1/2/3-day deterministic forecasts plus experimental 5–10 day products developed with partners (RIMES; Google; earlier Georgia Tech/CFAB). The public site typically shows only the current/recent season; long historical daily series generally require a formal request to BWDB's Hydrology Directorate rather than being downloadable. There is **no clearly stated open-data license** — treat scraped values as government data requiring attribution, and check the site footer/terms page. Practical scraping approach: (i) open the `/app/` dashboard, inspect the Network tab for XHR/fetch JSON endpoints, (ii) if JSON endpoints exist, hit them directly with polite rate limiting; (iii) otherwise parse the legacy HTML station tables and archive the daily PDF bulletins. Community GitHub scrapers reportedly exist (search `github FFWC scraper water level`), but specific repositories need verification.

**Complementary free, redistributable sources to fuse:** NASA POWER (public domain), CHIRPS (open), IMERG/GPM satellite precipitation, ERA5/ERA5-Land (Copernicus — open with attribution), GloFAS reanalysis/forecasts, the Google Flood Hub API, Sentinel-1 SAR and MODIS (Copernicus/NASA — open), and HDX humanitarian datasets. Because NASA POWER is public domain and CHIRPS is open, your two core CSVs are freely redistributable — which is exactly what makes a dataset/benchmark paper legally clean. BMD normals PDFs and any FFWC-scraped data carry the most licensing uncertainty and should not be redistributed without confirming terms.

### What each of your datasets enables
- `bgd-rainfall-subnat-full.csv` (CHIRPS/WFP dekadal, admin-1/2, ~1981–present): rainfall nowcasting/seasonal targets, anomaly/quantile (rfq/r1q/r3q) drought indices, and the "rainfall" track of BanglaWeatherBench. Strongest for drought/anomaly and teleconnection work.
- `All_Districts_Combined.csv` (NASA POWER daily, multi-district, multi-decade): the workhorse for BanglaWeatherBench (multi-variable daily station-like series) and the *complete* WBGT feature set. Soil-moisture fields (GWETTOP/GWETROOT/GWETPROF) additionally enable antecedent-soil-moisture features for agricultural drought / flood conditioning.
- `Hazard_Weather.xls`: potential labels for supervised extreme-event / hazard classification if it contains dated disaster records — inspect first.
- BMD normals PDFs: station-level climatological baselines to define anomalies and to sanity-check reanalysis-derived series.
- Planned FFWC scrape: the only path to river-stage targets for a flood-forecasting or Flood Hub-audit paper.

### Ranked shortlist of paper topics

**#1 — BanglaWeatherBench: Zero-Shot & Few-Shot Time-Series Foundation Models on Tropical Monsoon Station Data.**
Novelty claim: *No published benchmark systematically evaluates zero-shot/few-shot time-series foundation models against classical baselines on tropical-monsoon, data-scarce South Asian meteorological station series, with error explicitly stratified by monsoon regime and extremes.* Data: `All_Districts_Combined.csv` (daily multi-variable, multi-district) + `bgd-rainfall-subnat` (dekadal). Methods: Chronos, TimesFM, Moirai, TTM, Moment (zero-shot; light LoRA/fine-tune where supported) vs. seasonal-naive, ARIMA, XGBoost, and a small LSTM/PatchTST. Metrics: MASE, CRPS (probabilistic), RMSE, monsoon-vs-dry-season skill, extreme-event conditional error, per-district breakdown. Expected figures/tables: leaderboard table; skill-vs-horizon curves; monsoon/dry seasonal decomposition; extreme-conditional error bars; per-variable (T2M, RH2M, rainfall) panels. Compute: inference-only on one GPU. Time: 4–5 weeks. **Novelty 9 / Feasibility 9 / Impact 8.**

**#2 — An Independent Audit of Google Flood Hub in Bangladesh.**
Novelty claim: *No independent third-party evaluation of Google Flood Hub river forecasts against FFWC observed water levels exists.* Data: Flood Hub API forecasts + scraped FFWC water levels + FFWC's own 1/3-day forecasts. Methods: skill scoring (CSI, POD, FAR, reliability diagrams, lead-time-resolved error) vs. persistence and vs. FFWC operational forecasts. Metrics/figures: threshold-based hit/miss vs. Danger Level, ROC, lead-time reliability, station map of skill. Compute: near-zero. Time: 4–6 weeks. Key risk: FFWC historical-data access and Flood Hub API coverage. **Novelty 10 / Feasibility 6 / Impact 9.**

**#3 — ML for Wet-Bulb/WBGT Heat-Humidity Compound Extremes in Bangladesh.**
Novelty claim: *No dedicated ML characterization/prediction of WBGT/wet-bulb extremes for Bangladesh despite its identification as a global wet-bulb hotspot.* Data: `All_Districts_Combined.csv` (all WBGT inputs present). Methods: derive WBGT/wet-bulb from T2M/RH2M/QV2M/radiation/wind; gradient boosting + lightweight DL for trend detection and short-horizon extreme prediction; SHAP attribution; trend/return-period analysis. Metrics/figures: extreme-day classification skill, trend maps by district, SHAP driver ranking, seasonal cycle of exceedances. Compute: low. Time: 4 weeks. **Novelty 8 / Feasibility 9 / Impact 8.**

**#4 — Caravan-GBM / CAMELS-BD Dataset Contribution.**
Novelty claim: *No large-sample hydrology dataset (Caravan/CAMELS family) covers the GBM/Bangladesh.* Best framed as a Caravan-format community extension: ERA5-Land forcing + FFWC water-level targets (note: stage, not discharge — state this limitation clearly). Compute: low. Time: 5–6 weeks. Bottleneck/risk: discharge/rating-curve availability; without discharge it is a "Caravan-style stage dataset," which is still publishable but weaker. **Novelty 9 / Feasibility 5 / Impact 9.**

**#5 — Conformal Prediction / Uncertainty Quantification for Flood Water-Level Forecasting in a Data-Scarce Basin.**
Novelty claim: *Conformal prediction is rarely applied to operational flood forecasting, and essentially unstudied in data-scarce South Asia.* Data: FFWC series + NASA POWER/CHIRPS antecedent (incl. soil-moisture) features. Methods: base forecaster (LSTM/XGBoost) + split/adaptive conformal prediction for calibrated intervals. Metrics: coverage vs. nominal, interval width, conditional coverage at high flows. Compute: low. Time: 4–5 weeks. **Novelty 8 / Feasibility 7 / Impact 7.**

**#6 — Causal Discovery of ENSO/IOD/MJO Teleconnections to Bangladesh Rainfall.**
Novelty claim: *Causal discovery (PCMCI/Granger) applied to Bangladesh monsoon teleconnections is underexplored.* Data: rainfall CSVs + public climate indices (Niño 3.4, DMI, MJO/RMM). Methods: PCMCI+, Granger, lagged causal graphs; compare to correlation baselines. Compute: low. Time: 4–5 weeks. **Novelty 7 / Feasibility 8 / Impact 7.**

**#7 — Spatio-Temporal GNN over the FFWC River-Gauge Network.**
Novelty claim: *GNNs over the GBM gauge network for multi-station water-level forecasting are largely uncovered.* Risk: requires the scraped FFWC network topology and more tuning/compute than #1–#3; harder to finish cleanly in 4–6 weeks. **Novelty 8 / Feasibility 6 / Impact 7.**

### Publication strategy and practicalities
- **arXiv endorsement:** cs.AI and cs.LG require endorsement for new submitters. The fastest route is a co-author or advisor already endorsed/publishing in the category (an endorser generally needs several recent papers in that archive); otherwise use arXiv's endorsement request flow. Verify current endorsement thresholds on arXiv's help pages before submitting.
- **Cross-listing:** submit cs.LG (or cs.AI) as primary and cross-list physics.ao-ph to reach the climate/atmosphere audience; for a benchmark, adding cs.LG maximizes discoverability.
- **Reproducibility/licensing:** release code on GitHub and mint a dataset DOI via Zenodo or HuggingFace Datasets under CC-BY. NASA POWER is public domain and CHIRPS is open, so those derived products are safe to redistribute; do **not** redistribute BMD or FFWC-scraped data without confirming terms — link to the source instead.
- **Venues beyond arXiv:** Tackling Climate Change with ML (CCAI) workshops at NeurIPS/ICLR/ICML; NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks track; AAAI AI for Social Impact; EGU/AGU; and journals Environmental Data Science, Artificial Intelligence for the Earth Systems (AIES), Journal of Hydrology, and Water Resources Research. Confirm 2026/2027 deadlines directly from each venue's site, as they shift annually.
- **Pre-empting reviewer criticism:** the recurring rejection reasons for regional weather-ML papers are (1) "just an LSTM on a local dataset," (2) weak/no strong baselines, (3) no operational comparison, (4) no uncertainty. BanglaWeatherBench structurally addresses all four: it is a benchmark (not a single model), includes strong classical and foundation-model baselines, reports probabilistic metrics (CRPS), and — if you fuse in FFWC/Flood Hub — an operational comparison.
